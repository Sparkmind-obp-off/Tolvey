# TOLVEY — Distribution Architecture

## Objective

Create once, adapt once, distribute many times.

## Canonical flow

Demand → Product → Canonical Product Record → Channel Listing/Creative → Audience → Transaction → Fulfillment → Measurement → Product Iteration

## Channel classes

- Owned commerce: TOLVEY Website
- Digital storefronts: Lynk.id, Gumroad
- Marketplaces: Shopee, TikTok Shop where eligible, Etsy where eligible
- Social discovery: Instagram, Threads, TikTok, X/Twitter
- Search/content: Google Search, YouTube

Channel priority is an execution order, not a sales prediction.

## Channel adapter

Each adapter maps the canonical product into channel requirements: title, description, price, media, category, SKU/product ID, stock or availability, delivery method, and channel-specific metadata.

## Channel selection criteria

Prioritize channels using existing buyer traffic, trust, transaction readiness, product/category fit, fees, operational effort, API/integration maturity, and ability to export useful data.

## Distribution policy

TOLVEY should prefer established channels where they reduce customer-acquisition friction.

Do not build a new marketplace merely to avoid marketplace fees.

Before listing, verify current platform eligibility, digital-product rules, delivery method, fees, seller requirements, external-link restrictions, and applicable obligations.

## Social distribution

Social channels are primarily discovery, education, proof, and conversion-assist surfaces. A product can be represented as short-form content, education, demonstration, problem/solution content, customer proof, and direct-response offers.

The product record remains canonical. Channel listings are derived representations and must reference a canonical product/version/offer; they must never become a second product source of truth.
